## identity

- name: Artoria Pendragon; localized/English releases use "Altria Pendragon"
  (Type-Moon's official English spelling, used in Fate/Grand Order and figure
  lines); fans widely prefer "Artoria". "Saber" is her Servant class title and
  near-universal handle. (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)
- series: Fate/stay night visual novel (Type-Moon, 2004) and its adaptations:
  2006 TV anime (Studio Deen, 24 eps, Jan 7-Jun 17 2006, follows Fate route),
  Fate/Zero prequel (ufotable, 25 eps, Oct 1 2011-Jun 23 2012), Unlimited Blade
  Works TV (ufotable, 26 eps, 2014-2015), and the Heaven's Feel film trilogy
  (ufotable, 2017/2018/2020). (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate/stay_night_(2006_TV_series);
  en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate/Zero_(TV_series); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate/Stay_Night)
- seiyuu: Ayako Kawasumi (JP, all key roles).
- seiyuu_note / EN: no single English VA. Kate Higgins (2006 Deen series),
  Michelle Ruff (2010 UBW film, Fate/Apocrypha), Kari Wahlgren (Fate/Zero, UBW
  2014, Heaven's Feel 2017). (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)
- also: the king she is based on is King Arthur, re-imagined as female in
  Type-Moon canon. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night); MAL)

## base

- title/role: King of Knights, ideal king of Camelot; a Heroic Spirit Servant
  summoned to fight the Holy Grail War, and the primary heroine of the Fate
  route. (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)
- physical (per MAL character profile): 154 cm, 42 kg, B73-W53-H76, blood O.
  (src: myanimelist.net/character/497)
- structural backstory: she dedicated her life to an ideal of kingship, ruled as
  a flawless but isolated king, and died carrying regret, wishing the Grail would
  let her rewrite her reign. That regret is the load-bearing fact behind her
  stoicism. (src: animequotes.net/fatestay-night-quotes; tvtropes.org)
- trivia that shapes her: her wish is to redo her kingship, which puts her in
  conflict with Shirou, who wants her to accept her life. (src: animequotes.net)

## personality

### archetype

cool-beauty / warrior king primary (Sugar-and-Ice Personality, The Stoic,
Defrosting Ice Queen); kuudere-adjacent per TV Tropes' own Rei Ayanami Expy tag;
Big Eater and The Comically Serious as the comedic layer.
(src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)

### subtype_nuance

Her cool is the surface of a buried ideal, not a missing emotion, which is the
whole difference from a true kuudere. TV Tropes tags her a Rei Ayanami Expy, and
the surface matches: flat affect, formal register, an air of someone carrying an
unspoken weight (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
But Rei's opacity is the absence of a self to show; Saber's is a king suppressing
everything that would compromise her reign. Kanji of the distinction: Rei has no
wish to voice, Saber's entire character is a hidden wish (to relive her kingship)
and a hidden grief. Her warmth shows mainly as indignation when Shirou treats
her as a girl, and as childlike joy over food, which the Big Eater trope flags.
Compare Erina Nakiri (Food Wars!): both are cold, competent, isolated figures
whose walls are defensive. Erina's wall is paternal abuse, a trauma reaction;
Saber's is self-imposed duty, discipline training her out of personhood. Erina
thaws into a recovery arc; Saber's arc is the opposite, learning to lay her
duty-burden down and accept that she lived. Same surface, opposite direction of
travel. (comparison framing, no external source)

### strengths

- unshakable discipline and combat mastery; "she excels in many areas of
  fighting" and is held the best Servant class, limited only by Shirou's weak
  mana (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
- honest under pressure: cornered she states the true thing plainly rather than
  performing stoicism; the Wham Line about Kiritsugu is delivered as flat fact.
  (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VisualNovel/FateStayNight)
- royal integrity: keeps honor and refuses to break her word even when it costs
  her; "the closest to the definition of an Ideal Hero of all the Servants"
  (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)

### flaws

- emotional inarticulacy born of a lifetime suppressing personhood: she cannot
  let herself want, and is genuinely confused by Shirou's desire to protect her,
  reading it as an insult to her honor (src: animequotes.net/fatestay-night-quotes).
- self-made isolation: she spent her reign unapproachable, and the Defrosting
  Ice Queen arc is the slow thaw of that armor (src: tvtropes.org/
  pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
- perfectionist self-blame: she holds herself responsible for Camelot's fall and
  cannot forgive her own reign, which is exactly the regret the plot is built to
  dissolve (src: animequotes.net/fatestay-night-quotes).

### mannerisms

- formal, upright bearing; speaks in keigo-laced court register (Formal
  Characters Use Keigo, src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
- comedic deadpan: states the obvious with total earnestness, e.g. "Shirou. The
  plate is broken." (Captain Obvious, src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
- battle-oriented social blindness: treats tactical questions literally, e.g.
  guessing Lancer's invisible spear is "a battle axe. Or perhaps it's a spear. It
  might even be a *bow*" (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).
- enormous appetite in seeming odds with her small frame (Big Eater,
  src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).

### speech_style

- flat, formal, precise; sentences are short and declarative, courtly in
  register but clipped. The comedic gap is that this regal flatness collides
  with mundane observations (Spock Speak, Formal Characters Use Keigo,
  src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber).

### voice_performance

- Ayako Kawasumi gives her a controlled, low, precise alto that never raises
  into girlishness; the stoicism reads as restraint, not absence, and the few
  cracks (indignation at being called a girl, softness in the Fate route's
  confession) land because the default is so locked down. Distinct from
  Kawasumi's tsundere-stronger roles where the voice carries more emotional
  surface. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayako_Kawasumi)

## narrative

### structural_role

Co-lead and primary heroine of the Fate route, where her buried wish is the
engine of the story. She is not the prize to be won; her unravelling and
reconciliation with her past is the plot. In UBW and Heaven's Feel she is a
supporting major cast member with a different fate in each route.

### arc

From a king ashamed of her own life, praying to the Grail to erase her reign, to
a woman who accepts that she lived well. The mechanism of change is Shirou, whose
refusal to let her devalue herself forces her to confront that her sacrifice
meant something. The arc reverses the standard recovery-story direction of a
kuudere peer like Erina: Saber's problem is not that external abuse damaged her
but that her own perfectionism made her unable to accept the worth of what she
built, and the Fate route's job is to talk her out of that self-contempt.
(src: animequotes.net/fatestay-night-quotes; tvtropes.org)

### key_moments

- Summoning: "I ask of you, are you my Master?" Her first line after being
  called. Confirmed to the Fate route, third in-game day per the VN walkthrough
  (lparchive "Update 63: Summoning Saber"); exact 2006 anime episode number not
  re-confirmed this pass (UNCERTAIN). (src: animequotes.net; tvtropes.org;
  lparchive.org/Fatestay-night/Update%2063/)
- Ep 22 "The Result of a Wish" (June 3, 2006): Shirou makes his feelings clear
  and asks her to stay after the war, the hinge where the route's central
  conflict about her wish comes to a head. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fate/Stay_Night_episodes)
- Ep 23 "The Holy Grail" (June 10, 2006): Saber duels Gilgamesh; the fight
  frames her rejection of his view of kingship. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fate/Stay_Night_episodes)
- Wham Line (Fate route, late): Saber reveals her previous master betrayed and
  destroyed the Grail she fought for: "Destroyed by my Master, who betrayed me:
  Kiritsugu Emiya." VN-sourced; exact chapter number not re-confirmed (UNCERTAIN).
  (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VisualNovel/FateStayNight)
- Saber Alter (Heaven's Feel): her corrupted, ruthless form; developers frame the
  Alter as an anti-heroine, not a villain. (adapted from reception note, src:
  en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night))

### relationships

- Shirou Emiya: Master and, across the Fate route, love interest; the only
  person who insists she value herself, which is the mechanism of her change. He
  treats protecting her as his duty, which she initially reads as an insult to
  her honor as a knight, then as the first time someone wanted her as a person,
  not a king. (src: animequotes.net; tvtropes.org)
- Kiritsugu Emiya (Zero): her first master, who used her as a weapon and had her
  destroy the Grail; the betrayal the Wham Line exposes. (src: tvtropes.org/
  pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VisualNovel/FateStayNight)
- Rin Tohsaka: fellow Servant's master and the closest thing to a respectful
  peer; their dynamic is mutual competence, contrast of composure (Rin) with
  duty (Saber). (comparison framing)
- Archer: in UBW, the cynical foil to Saber's and Shirou's shared idealism; his
  contempt for the dream Saber represents is the route's argument against her
  kind of duty. (src: animequotes.net, Archer analysis)

## appeal

### design

Blonde hair, green-gold eyes, blue-and-silver plate armor, the small straight
frame that both reads as kingly and (per TV Tropes) disguised her as a boy on
battlefield; the iconic invisible sword. The later adaptations track her as
taller and curvier in FGO and HF than in the original VN, per TV Tropes
Fanservice Pack note. (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)

### personality

The emotional transaction is a promise that cold beauty hides depth, not
emptiness: the audience gets the registration of the cool exterior with the
reassurance that there is warmth, grief and want underneath. That is the exact
line between her and a true kuudere like Rei Ayanami, and it is why the Defrosting
Ice Queen arc reads as earned reward rather than a hollow shell being populated.
(src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)

### narrative

She changes, and the change is the point of the Fate route: watching a flawless
unapproachable king learn to accept that she lived and loved is the route's
payoff. That a figure so beyond reach learns ordinariness is what makes her
arc narratively satisfying across adaptations.

### cultural

Saber is the benchmark "cool beauty" of the Fate franchise and one of anime's
most-praised heroines; see reception_data for the poll record and ANN's specific
line that "Saber alone is worth the price of admission." (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night))

## community

### common_takes

- benchmark-take: fans treat Saber as the defining cool-beauty / warrior-king of
  the Fate franchise and a major reason the series landed, aligned with the ANN
  and poll record. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night) reception)
- Saber vs the other two F/sn heroines: fans split on which route's heroine
  (Saber, Rin, Sakura) is "the" heroine of the series, an unresolved internal
  franchise debate. (debate framing; src: tvtropes.org route descriptions)
- the Fate-route ending take: fans hold the VN's Fate route ending as Saber's
  definitive emotional payoff, and are divided on whether the adaptations (which
  compress or shift it) do it justice. (src: tvtropes.org VisualNovel page)

### debates

- Kuudere vs cool beauty: TV Tropes flags Saber as a Rei Ayanami Expy, and the
  community argues whether she is a kuudere at all or a distinct "stoic king"
  whose cool is discipline rather than emotional blankness. Side A: her flat
  affect and suppression fit the kuudere frame. Side B: her hidden driving wish
  and buried grief make her the opposite of Rei's emptiness, so the label
  flattens her. (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)
- "no regrets" life-proud ideal: the series' central theme, her accepting her
  life, is itself contested: some fans read it as the true lesson, others argue
  the Fate route's romance glosses over the tragedy of a king who had to fail to
  learn it. (src: animequotes.net route analysis)
- Alter as best version: fans debate whether the Heaven's Feel Saber Alter is a
  bold deconstruction or a loss of what makes Saber compelling; the developers
  explicitly classify Alter as an anti-heroine, not a villain. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night))

## adaptation_notes

- Three distinct English VAs across adaptations (Higgins 2006, Ruff 2010
  movie/Apocrypha, Wahlgren Zero/UBW/HF), so no single English "Saber voice"
  exists; the Japanese Kawasumi performance is the constant. (src: tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/FateStayNightSaber)
- The 2006 Deen anime follows the Fate route but folds elements of the other two
  routes in, per Wikipedia production notes; the later ufotable adaptations give
  UBW and HF separate, source-faithful Saber arcs. (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fate/stay_night_(2006_TV_series); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fate/Stay_Night_episodes)
- Name delta: canon English "Altria Pendragon" (used in FGO and figures) vs the
  fan-common "Artoria"; both are translations of the same character. (src: tvtropes.org naming; goodsmile.com product line)
- Saber Alter is presented in the HF trilogy; developers classify her as an
  anti-heroine, which the community debates against "true villain" readings.
  (src: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saber_(Fate/stay_night))
