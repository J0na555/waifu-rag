## identity

- name: Kurumi Tokisaki (時崎狂三). Name encodes the character: kuru = "crazy",
  toki = "time" (src: tvtropes Meaningful Name; fandom)
- show: Date A Live. Light novels by Koushi Tachibana, illustrations by Tsunako,
  Fujimi Fantasia Bunko, Vol 1 "Tohka Dead End" March 19, 2011, 22 volumes
  ending 2020 (src: fandom; tvtropes; Wikipedia). Anime Season 1: 12 eps,
  April 6 - June 22, 2013, AIC Plus+, dir. Keitaro Motonaga (src: fandom;
  Wikipedia)
- seiyuu: Asami Sanada (JP), Alexis Tipton (EN dub) (src: MAL; tvtropes; fandom)

## base

- birthday: June 10 (Gemini). Spirit number 3, the Third Spirit (src: MAL; fandom)
- age: presented as a teenage girl (roughly 17-18 physically); true age is
  higher — has existed and respawned across many years (at least 30+ per canon
  past), listed as "Unknown" (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- height: 157 cm (src: MAL; fandom)
- role: first Spirit presented as a real antagonist; the "can't seal her"
  outlier among the sealed harem (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- structural fact: her Angel Zafkiel runs on her own time — every use consumes
  her lifespan, refilled by consuming other people's time. Personal kill count
  reported at over 10,000, "not including spacequake victims," per Mana
  Tobiichi's intel (src: MAL; fandom; tvtropes). Load-bearing: the time economy
  is the leash she never solves and the engine of her hunting.

## personality

### archetype

yandere, PREDATOR subtype: curated, composed menace. Where Yuno is the classic
subtype (love is the whole of her, the devotion is total and the violence
follows from it), Kurumi performs menace as a persona. She curtsies, uses keigo,
calls people "-san", and speaks of murder with the same pleased politeness she
uses to invite someone to tea. Violence is a wardrobe she can take off, not a
fracture. The killer's politeness is the act; the clockwork eye and the
"kihihihi" laugh are the tells that the act is staged. The word "yandere" needs
the subtype qualifier: the question is genuinely contested, see
community.debates (src: fandom; tvtropes; villains wiki).

### subtype_nuance

Three-way contrast the Analyst must hold:

- Yuno (classic): love is the total cause. The violence, the world-ending, the
  objects of devotion, all bend to one person. No agenda outside the beloved.
  Endgame is being with him.
- Esdeath (predator-possessive glamour): loves the strong man as a prize,
  displays him, dominates him; the cruelty is identified with a conqueror's
  self. Her ownership is sincere, her affection possessive rather than
  instrumental.
- Kurumi (staged/theatrical, self-possessed): the menace is a role she runs on
  purpose. Her real motive is not possession of Shido and not romance. She
  wants his accumulated Spirit power to fuel a trip 30 years back to murder the
  First Spirit (Mio Takamiya) and erase the spacequake tragedy, herself
  included. Obsession with Shido is framed by TV Tropes as largely
  instrumental: she tries to kill him "with the same excitement" she speaks of
  "becoming one" with him. On the predator reading, her curated "big sister"
  persona is cover for that instrumentality: every tender line is on the clock
  (src: tvtropes Yandere; fandom; villains wiki; animearchetypes tumblr).

### strengths

- composed under any provocation; the polite mask holds while she is killing
- strategic layer: runs a network of shadow clones gathering intel worldwide;
  clones feed one mind, the original effectively impossible to pin down
  (src: fandom; tvtropes; villains wiki)
- effectively unkillable meanwhile: Zafkiel rewinds/replaces her; enemies kill
  her over and over and she returns (src: anisearch; fandom)
- willing to take huge, irreversible gambits for her goal, including rewriting
  her own past rather than sinking into darkness (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- genuine protective streak under the menace: soft spot for cats and children;
  she will cross people who hurt them (src: tvtropes Pet the Dog; fandom)

### flaws

- the time-consumption economy: every Zafkiel use costs lifetime, refilled by
  consuming others' time; her own power is the leash she never solves
  (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- the self-possession is a load-bearing lie: she repeatedly rewound herself to
  avoid facing what she did to her best friend Sawa Yamauchi — the coldness is
  armor, not absence (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- instrumental view of people leaks through and undercuts trust; her own clones
  at the school roof nearly broke rank and let Shido save them, a crack in the
  persona (src: tvtropes; villains wiki)
- cannot reach her stated goal alone: no matter how far she jumps, she can't go
  30 years back without more energy; that is the whole engine of her hunting
  (src: fandom)

### mannerisms

- left eye is a golden clock/pocket-watch face; the number the gun hand rests
  on chooses which Zafkiel bullet fires (src: tvtropes; fandom; villains wiki)
- two-gun stance, flintlock and musket as hour/minute hands (src: tvtropes;
  fandom)
- "Ara ara" catchphrase (src: tvtropes Catchphrase)
- "Kihihihi" evil laugh (src: tvtropes Evil Laugh)
- curtsies when greeting, including mid-menace (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- long bangs hide the left eye; the clock face shows only in Spirit form
  (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- fan-registered signature tics (the "one, two, three" countdown before firing;
  holding her own hand; repeated dinner invitations) are widely repeated in fan
  material but could not be verified to a transcript this session. They go in
  the hunt list, not the verified section.

### speech_style

Formal, keigo-laden "refined lady" register ("watakushi", "-san"), even while
threatening or killing; the politeness itself is the menace. Uses the coy
"my, my" and upspeak. Contrast with the dead-flat registers of a Taiga or a
Yuno just before the snap: Kurumi never raises the mask at all (src: tvtropes;
fandom; villains wiki).

### voice_performance

Asami Sanada reads her in the refined-lady register with clockwork calm, and
the "kihihihi" laugh is specifically praised by fans as the moment the mask
taps the fourth wall ("Kurumi's crazy laugh is famous, and her voice actress is
praised for her portrayal" — anisearch/anibase trivia). Contrast role: Vita in
Magical Girl Lyrical Nanoha — the same throaty politeness for a lethal girl is
a pattern in Sanada's career, not a one-off (src: anisearch; anibase).

## narrative

### structural_role

Deuteragonist-turned-anti-hero and the series' primary "villain who is on the
right side of history" engine. The first Spirit introduced as a real
antagonist; the petri dish where the series argues whether it is a romance of
sealing or a story about untrustworthy memory and who deserves to die. She is
the agent who keeps Shido's story honest by refusing the seal, and who arrives
late each season to break the status quo (src: fandom; tvtropes).

### arc

From menace ("Worst Spirit") to trickster-ally to revealed crusader whose goal
is her own erasure. Vol 3 / S1 eps 7-10: fights Mana, Tohka, Origami, wins,
loses to Kotori's Camael, retreats. Mid-series: ally when it serves her goal.
The reveal (mostly LN Vol 16, adapted in later seasons): she was a kind girl
tricked by Mio Takamiya into becoming a Spirit, accidentally killed her best
friend Sawa Yamauchi, and her whole campaign is to go back far enough to erase
Mio's birth and undo the Spirits, herself included. She re-ran the day Shido
nearly died 204 times to save him. Resolution: halts a First-Spirit-linked
catastrophe, fixes the 5-year rewind her 12th bullet caused, then sends Shido
back to correct it (src: tvtropes; fandom; anibase).

### key_moments

- Transfer-in / reveal ("The Visitors," S1 ep 7, May 2013, LN vol 3): the
  polite transfer student quiets the room; the audience knows the worst is here
  (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- Shadow army + spacequake threat ("Nightmare of Madness," S1 ep 9, June 2013,
  LN vol 3): her Worst-Spirit power as large-scale lease and real threat
  (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- Defeat by Kotori's Camael ("Spirit of Flame (Efreet)," S1 ep 10): establishes
  she is beatable, so she has to be slyer (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- Rooftop scene where a clone nearly lets Shido save her; "pet the dog" reveal
  that under the killer is the girl who fed a kitten (src: tvtropes; villains
  wiki)
- LN Vol 16 / later-season reveal: she has rewound her own day over 200 times
  to keep Shido alive; her best friend's blood set the crusade in motion
  (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- The "Countdown" episode titles (S1 ep 11 "Countdown") nod at her tic
  (src: fandom; IMDB)

## relationships

- Shido Itsuka: instrumental first, genuine later. She wants his sealed power
  to fuel the time-jump; TV Tropes frames "becoming one" and kill-him-with-the-
  same-excitement as goal-driven, not plain romance. Later seasons and LN show
  real affection growing (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- Origami Tobiichi: a mirror, not a rival. Both want to reach the past; Kurumi
  uses her 12th bullet to send Origami back 5 years, then cleans up the worse
  outcome she caused, sending Shido back to fix it (src: fandom; anibase)
- Mana Takamiya: same world, opposite side; Mana has killed her repeatedly,
  Kurumi keeps returning (src: anibase; fandom)
- Tohka and the sealed harem: never one of them; the one who cannot be sealed,
  which is her whole point (src: fandom; tvtropes)
- Mio Takamiya (the First Spirit): the reason she exists and the target of her
  entire campaign (src: tvtropes; fandom)
- Her clones: herself, treated with a strange mixture of command and
  tenderness; clones reflect the softer self she suppressed, and a clone Shido
  was kind to starts to feel it (src: fandom; villains wiki; tvtropes)

## appeal

### design

Full elegant-gothic-lolita nightmare aesthetic: crimson and black, clockwork
eye, twin tails, long bangs, two guns, a giant clock Angel. Her visual identity
is the same costume as her menace: beautiful and on purpose, with one visible
malfunction (the eye) as the tell. The bangs-hide-the-eye, reveal-in-Spirit-
form button is a deliberate, repeatable design beat. The name ("crazy time")
is baked into the costume (src: fandom; tvtropes; villains wiki).

### personality

The appeal is the tension, not the warmth: a killer so composed she curtsies,
so thorough she reads all the intel, who cares about cats and children and
whose stated goal is to erase tragedy including herself. Fans read her as the
"coolest" of the cast precisely because she never breaks register even while
killing. The predator register is the selling point; the softness under it is
a trickle, not a flood (src: tvtropes; fandom; anibase).

### narrative

She is the only hero who refuses the series' central premise (seal-and-date).
The show argues its own morality through her: every season she shows up late
and changes the board, and the series visibly trusts her to carry the plot
when it needs a jolt. The LN Vol 16 backstory retroactively makes her
extermination crusade the correct one (Mio caused 150 million deaths), which
is exactly the moral framing that stirs the whitewash debate below
(src: tvtropes; fandom).

### cultural

On the yandere spectrum she is the most-referenced "predator" case in fandom
conversation: cited in "scariest yandere" listicles and in subtype debates as
the canonical "not actually a yandere, actually a yangire/predator with a
mission." Break-out character with her own light novel and anime spin-off
(Date A Bullet) (src: screenrant via search; yan-dere wikis; fandom).

## community

### common_takes

- "Best girl / the goat of Date A Live": common framing on r/datealive; polls
  and mywaifulist ranks place her top-2 with Tohka; some fans argue her
  complexity makes her the better lead (src: reddit; mywaifulist)
- "The coolest character": the mask vs the softness, the composed predator, is
  the mainstream appeal (src: anisearch; reddit)
- The "actually heroic" reading: she is on the right side of the First Spirit
  catastrophe; the LN reveal proves her crusade was aimed at the true villain
  (src: tvtropes; reddit; villains wiki)
- The "she murders thousands, the franchise whitewashes her" counter-reading:
  her 10,000+ personal kills are real, victims are mostly off-screen, and the
  series' sympathy for her softens the body count; an r/datealive thread
  wrestles with her being a minor killing minors to reframe empathy
  (src: reddit)

### debates

- "Is she a yandere at all?" For: TV Tropes' Yandere entry places her there;
  yan-dere fan wiki; yandere listicles; the "crazy but still cute" power
  fantasy. Against: the anime archetypes tumblr and r/datealive argue her
  obsession is instrumental, not romantic and not an endgame (a yandere's
  endgame is being with the person; hers is a 30-year time murder), and propose
  yangire/predator or a bespoke "mayadere" (starts villain, becomes anti-hero);
  one thread argues Mukuro Hoshimiya, who memory-wiped everyone to monopolize
  Shido, is the actual yandere. No verdict (src: tvtropes; animearchetypes
  tumblr; reddit "Why I think Kurumi isn't a Yandere"; "Why do people call
  kurumi a yandere").
- "Kurumi vs Yuno, who owns the archetype?" Classic side: Yuno demonstrates
  the definition completely; the devotion is total, means and ends are one,
  no agenda separates love from violence. Predator side: Kurumi is the more
  watchable and more resonant case for a modern audience because the menace is
  curated and self-aware, a performance she can switch off; "crazy but still
  cute" is the whole act. Neither verdict proven; debate, not resolution
  (src: this dossier, citing the sources above).
